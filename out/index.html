What the debt is owed to

A debt is a money debt of €25.000 or more, from a separate disclosure filed with the same election papers. Short-term debts repaid within fourteen days, and administrative debts, need not be registered, and a natural-person creditor is published by initials, surname and place only. Debt is not income and is never added to gifts: a party that campaigns on credit and a party that is donated to are funded by different mechanisms.

c · What is owed to media and advertising suppliers

This is debt, not spending. It counts the media and advertising bills of €25.000 or more that a party had not yet paid when it filed. A party that settled its invoices on time shows nothing here, and a party that paid most of a large bill shows only the remainder, so every figure is a floor. It is published because it is the one comparable, dated, thresholded number that exists for campaign media money: for the final three weeks before the vote it is the only one. The neveninstellingen layer

gifts + NFRP

a · Entities that received gifts

Distinct non-null entity values from the gift edges, exactly as published and not merged: variants of one organisation appear as separate rows. The party is read only from the partij field on the same edge.

b · NFRP state money, chapter V

NFRP is the Nederlands Fonds voor Regionale Partnerschappen, the fund that carries the old MATRA programme. Its "Politieke Partijen" window sits in chapter V under Buitenlandse Zaken, artikel Veiligheid en stabiliteit, onderdeel "Bevordering van transitie in prioritaire gebieden", as a Subsidies (regelingen) instrument. It is the one place in the open register where the state names a party's own foundation as the recipient, which is why it matters here: the Wfpp party subsidy stops at the party, and the passthrough to its neveninstellingen is invisible.

Amounts in € thousand, grouped by KvK. The register spells one recipient several ways, so rows sharing a KvK are one entity; where a KvK is missing, the name stands alone and no merge is attempted. Where the register repeats an entity within a year, those rows are summed for that entity-year.

c · What the named entities actually do

The reports name these entities and their amounts and almost never say what they are for, so these descriptions come from the entity's own charter, year plan or site. Where the name settles it, the entry says so rather than implying a source.
