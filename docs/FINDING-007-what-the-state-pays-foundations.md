# Finding 007 — what the state pays party foundations, and through which scheme

Vintage: `data/aerarium.duckdb`, `recipient_line`, distinct rows, all years served (2016–2025). Amounts in
€ thousand. Source: rijksfinancien.nl/open-data, CC-0. Generated by `scripts/thinktanks.py` into
`out/thinktanks.json`.

An entity total is not a scheme total. This finding publishes both, because one scheme can be a partnership fund
with a political purpose and another can be a general subsidy line, and a single number lets one stand in for the
other.

## Totals

| class | entities | rows | €k |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| policy institutes | 5 | 77 | 77.705 |
| party-international foundations | 11 | 75 | 7.753 |

Five policy institutes carry €77.705k: Clingendael, ProDemos, the Anne Frank Stichting, HCSS and Rathenau.
Eleven party-international foundations carry €7.753k. These are **payments recorded in the recipient register**,
which is a register of what the budget says was paid and to whom. It is not a statement of what those bodies do
with the money, and this project publishes no purpose for any of them.

## The scheme split, which is the point

The eleven party-international foundations' €7.753k breaks down by recorded scheme into three groups:

| recorded scheme | rows | €k |
|---|---:|---:|
| named `NFRP Politieke Partijen%` | 46 | 3.289 |
| spelled out `Nederlands Fonds voor Regionale Partnerschappen` | 11 | 2.266 |
| other recorded schemes | 18 | 2.198 |

The two NFRP rows are **one fund printed two ways**. They are reported apart rather than merged, because merging
them would assert an identity this project has not verified against a scheme register, and because the difference
matters: a reader who sees only €3.289k under the NFRP label is reading a name match, not the fund's total. The
fund as a family is **€5.555k (57 rows)**.

**FICDD, worked as an example,** because it is the case an audit caught. Eight rows, €596k:

- `NFRP Politieke Partijen` €129k (2 rows), `NFRP Politieke Partijen 2023` €111k, `NFRP Politieke Partijen
  Programma 2022` €6k, `NFRP Politieke Partijen Programma 2018` €3k
- `Nederlands Fonds voor Regionale Partnerschappen` €149k (the same fund, spelled out)
- `Subsidieregeling BZ 2006` €155k
- `Matra overheid tot overheid 2020-2024` €43k

So €398k arrives through the NFRP family, €155k through the general BZ 2006 subsidy scheme, and €43k through
Matra. A single figure of €596k would have read as one programme. It is three.

**Clingendael shows the same defect in the scheme field:** `Subsidieregeling BZ 2006` €16.403k and `BZ 2006`
€5.215k are one scheme in two spellings, together €21.618k of its €44.769k. That is 48,3% of the institute's
recorded money under a scheme whose name the register does not spell consistently.

## Identity is the difficulty, not volume

Five entities, 152 rows in total. The work was not summing them, it was deciding which rows belong.

- **Nine rows named `Nederlandse Organisatie voor Technologie Ontwikkeling` carry Clingendael's KvK**, worth
  €24.703k. They are excluded: the name is TNO's, the register has misfiled the identifier. Excluding them is a
  judgement and it is recorded in `out/thinktanks.json` under `exclusion_ledger`, with the reason per row.
- **`Oud Clingendael Zorg B.V.`** is a care company, not the institute. Excluded.
- **Two Anne Frank Montessorischool rows** (€114k) share a name prefix and nothing else. Excluded.
- **Rathenau is matched by name only.** KvK 54667089 is KNAW's, and a KvK join on it sweeps in the Huygens
  Institute for €8,5m. One row, 2021, €224k, is what Rathenau shows.

## Where the named total is not the scheme total

ProDemos is the clean case. It is named in ten rows for **€26.040k**, but the scheme that carries its name also
publishes rows **without a recipient**: **€8.979k in 2022**, €5.266k in 2019, €4.403k in 2018 and €4.312k in 2017.
Clingendael has one such row, €812k in 2022.

Those amounts are reported beside the named total and **never added to it**, because the register does not say that
this money reached the entity. What they establish is narrower and still useful: on a scheme named for the
institute, the state publishes a large sum with the recipient withheld. So the named figure is what can be
**attributed**, not what the scheme spent, and the denominator a share would need is not in the register.

The join here is bounded deliberately. Matching anonymised rows on a **shared** scheme name — `Subsidieregeling BZ
2006`, which hundreds of recipients sit under — produced €1,203,081k for Clingendael in a single year. That is not a
conservative estimate, it is a false positive, and it is why only schemes whose name contains the entity's own name
are matched. The rule costs recall and buys the ability to state each figure without a caveat.

## What the register does not say

No row carries a purpose. Nothing here shows that any payment was made for a political position, and this project
does not infer one. A 2026 lane read the published material of 17 donor entities and found **none** publishing an
advocacy position, so the same rule applies on this side: money recorded, purpose not stated.

Where a scheme name contains a political term — `NFRP Politieke Partijen` — that is the scheme's own name. It is a
structural fact about the scheme, not a finding about any entity's activity, and it is reported as the name the
register prints.
