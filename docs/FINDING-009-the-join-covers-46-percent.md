# Finding 009 — the join covers 46% of expenditure, and the rest is vocabulary, not disclosure

Vintage 2026-11-26. Store `data/aerarium.duckdb`, read-only. Join at chapter × instrument type (`PROBE-010`).
Follows `FINDING-008`, which split 42 overshoots into 13 structural and 29 residual.

## The correction first

`FINDING-008` closed with the 29 residual groups, €9.053m, described as tractable: "each one is either a real
booking difference or a key collision". That was too optimistic, and the work below is what corrected it. The 29
do **not** close with an accounting identity inside this store. They fall into three classes, and only one class
can be closed here at all.

The classification, over all 42 overshoots (`out/overshoots.json`, generated by `scripts/overshoots.py`):

| class | groups | overshoot |
|---|---:|---:|
| outside the appropriation (premium- and fund-financed) | 13 | € 177.167,9 m |
| transfer structure | 16 | € 4.307,8 m |
| unexplained | 13 | € 4.744,9 m |

The first row is `FINDING-008`. The other two are the 29 residual groups, and the rest of this document is about
them.

## Class 1 — chapter-transfer lines are budget-heavy and register-light

A euro paid *to another chapter* is not a payment to a recipient, so a `Bijdrage aan (andere)
begrotingshoofdstukken` line has an appropriation and no recipient row. Where a chapter routes its money through
another chapter — the funds do this by design — the receiving chapter's register rows then exceed its own
appropriation line.

| year | chapter | overshoot on `Bijdrage aan medeoverheden` | transfer appropriation | transfer in register |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | XII | € 926.481 k | € 14.697.584 k | € 6.010.410 k |
| 2020 | XII | € 922.512 k | € 7.899.748 k | € 6.343.743 k |
| 2018 | XII | € 860.014 k | € 12.930.367 k | € 6.335.031 k |
| 2021 | XII | € 835.016 k | € 14.366.592 k | **€ 0** |

In 2021 chapter XII appropriates €14,4bn to transfer to other chapters and the register records **no** recipient
row for it, while the chapter's payments to medeoverheden exceed their own line by €835m. The money is in the
chapter and the payment is in the chapter, but they are not on the same instrument, and no query can pair them
without the receiving chapter's own line.

## Class 2 — the bigger number: the register's vocabulary covers 46% of expenditure

This is the part that reframes the whole measure. Across all eleven years, expenditure of **€ 7.392,5bn** splits:

| instrument label | expenditure | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| one of the ten instrument types the register uses | **€ 3.381,0bn** | **45,7%** |
| a policy instrument name the register never prints | € 4.000,6bn | 54,1% |
| blank or the literal string `0` | € 10,9bn | 0,1% |

The register's `instrument` column holds ten types (`Bekostiging`, `Bijdrage aan medeoverheden`, `Subsidies
(regelingen)` …). The budget's `instrument_naam` holds 286 values, most of which are policy lines —
`Functionele uitgaven van de Koning`, `Geneeskundige geestelijke gezondheidszorg`. **No recipient row can sit
under 54,1% of expenditure**, and their absence from the register is a naming difference, not a failure to
disclose. A reachability measure that counts those euros as "money that stopped at rung 3" is measuring the
budget's labelling convention.

## Class 3 — what is genuinely unexplained

Two of the large residual groups have **no** transfer offset at all — the chapter has no `Bijdrage aan (andere)
begrotingshoofdstukken` appropriation and no register rows for one:

- **VI 2022, `Bijdrage aan medeoverheden`** — € 1.634.121 k over, transfer line absent on both sides.
- **A 2018, `Bijdrage aan agentschappen`** — € 1.625.872 k over, transfer line absent on both sides.

These are the honest candidates for a booking difference or a key collision, and they are the only part of the 29
that further work inside this store could close.

## The column that would have bridged it is empty

`budget_line` carries `rbv_model`, which reads like the instrument classification that would map policy lines onto
register types. It is **blank in all 44.352 expenditure rows**, every year. That is the second such column in this
schema, after `beleid_of_niet_beleid`, and it means the bridge has to come from outside: the RBV instrument
classification as published, not as a column in the API.

## What this fixes about the project's claims

The grade distribution cannot cover 100% of expenditure. It covers the **45,7%** that carries a
register-compatible instrument label, and everything outside it must be graded *not comparable*, not *absent*.
That is a narrower claim than "every euro is graded", and it is the true one.

The 29 residual groups, in full, with their deltas, are in `out/overshoots.json` so a reader can check any of them
rather than take a class on trust.

## Not claimed

- That the residual groups are errors. Sixteen of the twenty-nine sit in chapters that route money through
  other chapters, which is a documented governance arrangement, not a mistake.
- That the 45,7% is a disclosure rate. It is a share of expenditure the two registers can *label* the same way.
  How much of the state's money reaches a named recipient is a separate question, and
  `PROBE-006` measured the naming side of it.
