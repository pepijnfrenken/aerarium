# Finding 009 — the join covers 48,6% of expenditure, and the rest is vocabulary, not disclosure

Vintage 2026-11-28. Store `data/aerarium.duckdb`, read-only. Join at chapter × instrument type (`PROBE-010`), on the
**realised account** (`fase='JV'`).

**Correction, 2026-11-28 — this supersedes the first version of this finding.** Two things were wrong in it. It
summed the draft budget and the realised account, so every "appropriation" was draft plus realised; and it filed
the literal string `0` with the policy labels, which put a missing marker into a bucket described as named policy
instruments. On the realised basis the joinable share is **48,6%**, not 45,7%, and the policy bucket is **50,4%**,
not 54,1%. The all-phase sum survives only as `diagnostic_all_phases` in `out/overshoots.json` and is not a
comparator.

## The join at this grain

**567** chapter-instrument-type years join. The register against the realised appropriation:

| bucket | groups |
|---|---:|
| at or below the appropriation | 328 |
| above it, but inside the 2% tolerance | 143 |
| above it by more than 2% | 96 |

The middle bucket is not "within budget". Previously all 533 of the first two were published as "at or below",
which mislabelled 143 groups the register puts *above* the line but close to it. The tolerance exists because
re-publication moves a line by small amounts; it does not turn an excess into a match, and the words now say so.

## The three classes, over all 96 groups

`out/overshoots.json`, generated by `scripts/overshoots.py`:

| class | groups | overshoot |
|---|---:|---:|
| outside the appropriation (premium- and fund-financed) | 18 | € 178.462,2 m |
| shares a chapter-year with a positive transfer appropriation | 35 | € 5.293,1 m |
| neither | 43 | € 8.352,2 m |

The first row is `FINDING-008`. The classes are interpretations laid over measured deltas: the artifact carries each
group's delta, both sides, and the transfer line's amounts and row counts, so a reader can disagree with a class
without recomputing the arithmetic.

## Class 1 — a transfer appropriation is not a traced edge

A euro paid *to another chapter* is not a payment to a recipient, so a `Bijdrage aan (andere)
begrotingshoofdstukken` line can carry an appropriation with no recipient row. **35** of the residual groups sit in
a chapter-year that has such a line. That is the whole of the claim: the presence of the line, not an edge from it.

| year | chapter | overshoot on `Bijdrage aan medeoverheden` | transfer appropriation | transfer in register |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | XII | € 926.481 k | € 14.697.584 k | € 6.010.410 k |
| 2020 | XII | € 922.512 k | € 7.899.748 k | € 6.343.743 k |
| 2018 | XII | € 860.014 k | € 12.930.367 k | € 6.335.031 k |
| 2021 | XII | € 835.016 k | € 14.366.592 k | no rows |

In 2021 chapter XII appropriates € 14,4bn to transfer to other chapters and the register records **no** recipient row
for it, while the chapter's payments to medeoverheden exceed their own line by € 835m. That is suggestive; it is not
a reconciliation, and four of the 35 are overshoots *on the transfer instrument itself*, which a transfer cannot
explain. All 35 are therefore left unexplained in financing terms.

## Three screens, three answers, and only one of them is a join

The 48,6% below is the **vocabulary** screen: it asks only whether an instrument string appears somewhere on both
sides, in any year and any chapter. It is a ceiling on comparability, not a join. Two stricter questions, on the same
realised universe (`out/overshoots.json`, `screens`):

| screen | expenditure comparable | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| vocabulary — the label appears on both sides | € 1.691,7bn | 48,6% |
| same key — year, chapter and type coincide | € 1.162,4bn | 33,4% |
| same key, excluding the two outside-appropriation types | € 843,0bn | 24,2% |

The gap between the first and second rows is 15,2 percentage points of expenditure that shares a *word* with the
register but not a *line*: same-type payments exist somewhere, in another year or another chapter. The third row
removes the premium- and fund-financed types, because a matched key there does not describe a matched flow. Any
claim about how much of expenditure can be followed must name which of these three it means; none of them is a euro
traced from an appropriation to a recipient.

## Class 2 — the register's vocabulary covers 48,6% of expenditure

On the realised account, expenditure of **€ 3.480,9bn** splits:

| instrument label | expenditure | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| one of the ten instrument types the register uses | **€ 1.691,7bn** | **48,6%** |
| a policy instrument name the register never prints | € 1.755,4bn | 50,4% |
| blank, or the literal string `0` | € 33,8bn | 1,0% |

The register's `instrument` column holds ten types (`Bekostiging`, `Bijdrage aan medeoverheden`, `Subsidies
(regelingen)` …). The budget's `instrument_naam` holds 286 values, most of which are policy lines —
`Functionele uitgaven van de Koning`, `Geneeskundige geestelijke gezondheidszorg`. **No recipient row can sit under
50,4% of expenditure**, and their absence from the register is a naming difference, not a failure to disclose. A
reachability measure that counts those euros as "money that stopped at rung 3" is measuring the budget's labelling
convention.

The `0` bucket matters for honesty in the other direction: a literal `0` is a missing marker, not a policy
instrument, and folding it into the policy bucket overstates how much of the state's money is labelled with a
deliberate scheme name.

## Class 3 — what is genuinely unexplained

**43** groups have neither a positive transfer appropriation in their chapter-year nor an outside-appropriation
instrument. Two of the largest:

- **VI 2022, `Bijdrage aan medeoverheden`** — € 1.634.121 k over, no transfer appropriation in that chapter-year.
- **A 2018, `Bijdrage aan agentschappen`** — € 1.625.872 k over, likewise.

Nonpositive baselines are carried rather than classified: XVII 2017 and 2018 have transfer appropriations of
−€ 272.332 k and −€ 35.922 k, and a negative baseline has no percentage-over-it meaning. Those rows keep their
signed delta and are flagged `budget_nonpositive` instead of being ordered by ratio.

## The column that would have bridged it is empty

`budget_line` carries `rbv_model`, which reads like the instrument classification that would map policy lines onto
register types. It is **blank in all 44.352 whole-row-distinct expenditure rows**, every year, and the column holds
no NULLs — the blank is the empty string. That is the second such column in this schema, after
`beleid_of_niet_beleid`, and it means the bridge has to come from outside: the RBV instrument classification as
published, not as a column in the API.

## What this fixes about the project's claims

The grade distribution cannot cover 100% of expenditure. It covers the **48,6%** that carries a register-compatible
instrument label, and everything outside it must be graded *not comparable*, never *absent*. That is a narrower
claim than "every euro is graded", and this finding is the correction that made it true.

The 96 groups, in full, with their deltas and transfer columns, are in `out/overshoots.json` so a reader can check
any of them rather than take a class on trust.

## Not claimed

- That the residual groups are errors. Thirty-five share a chapter-year with a transfer line and forty-three have
  nothing; neither state is a mistake and neither is a reconciliation.
- That 48,6% is a disclosure rate. It is a share of expenditure the two registers can *label* the same way. How much
  of the state's money reaches a named recipient is a separate question, and `PROBE-006` measured the naming side.
- That a label shared anywhere is a join. Two rows carrying the same instrument string in different years are not a
  matched key; that stricter screen is a separate measurement and is not claimed here.
