# LANE — Foreign state influence and extremist funding: what Dutch authorities have formally established

**Research date:** 2026-11-20  
**Mode:** read-only research. This lane wrote only this file. No git commit.  
**Scope:** (a) foreign state influence on Dutch politics; (b) funding of extremist or anti-democratic groups in the Netherlands; plus the three channels added by the scope correction: foreign funding of Dutch think tanks/foundations, foreign-state-linked lobbying registered at the Tweede Kamer, and foreign investment/ownership in Dutch media.

## 0. Verdict

The findings, in the order the sources support them:

1. **Russia** is the state most explicitly documented as conducting covert political influence *in the Netherlands*. The AIVD briefed Tweede Kamer members and Dutch MEPs on Russian covert influence; the MIVD recorded DDoS attacks on Dutch political-party websites during the 2024 European election. The AIVD’s 2023 report also says that “enkele andere landen” were active in the Netherlands to influence political decision-making, but does not name them.
2. **China** is documented as conducting political espionage and covert political influence abroad, and as using the Chinese diaspora in the Netherlands. The 2022 DBSA explicitly said there were then *no indications* of targeted Chinese influence against Dutch targets; later AIVD reports document the broader political-influence intent without naming a Dutch target.
3. **Gulf states** are documented as funders of mosques and religious institutions in the Netherlands, including Wahhabi-Salafi ones. The 2020 parliamentary interrogation (POCOB) quantified individual cases: As-Soennah €2.5m (2010–2017), alFitrah €1.4m (2011–2017), Essalam €8m. The AIVD reported in 2023 that this Gulf funding had become “nauwelijks” (barely) present.
4. **Turkey** is documented as paying and employing all Diyanet imams in the Netherlands and as having intervened in Dutch diaspora politics (the 2017 Rotterdam referendum campaign). No authority document in the examined corpus establishes Turkish funding of a Dutch political party.
5. **Iran** is documented as running cyber influence operations and transnational repression against diaspora critics. No authority document in the examined corpus establishes Iranian funding of a Dutch party, think tank or media outlet.
6. **The United States and Israel** are not named by any Dutch authority in the examined corpus as actors influencing Dutch politics. Their appearance is as geopolitical actors or allies, not as interference sources.
7. On **extremist funding**, AIVD 2025 documents a Hamas “political” network in Europe, with about ten people in the Netherlands, active in propaganda, lobbying and fundraising for Hamas in Gaza. The courts split on Al-Aqsa: a 2003 civil judgment accepted AIVD evidence that Al-Aqsa funds reached Hamas-linked organisations; a 2026 criminal judgment convicted the operator of a successor foundation for sanctions evasion and for continuing the banned Stichting Al Aqsa, but acquitted him of funding Hamas because the court could not establish that the money reached Hamas.
8. On the three added channels, the examined corpus contains **no authority document naming a Dutch think tank as foreign-funded**; the **Tweede Kamer’s only public lobby register lists passholders, not meetings, and contains no foreign state**; and **foreign media ownership recorded by the Commissariaat voor de Media is commercial (Belgian, German), not state ownership**.

This research found **no formal parliamentary inquiry (parlementaire enquête)** into foreign influence. POCOB was a parliamentary **interrogation** (ondervragingscommissie), not an enquiry.

## 1. Method, evidence standard, and limits

### 1.1 What was used

Primary documents only, downloaded and read as PDFs or official HTML:

- AIVD annual reports 2023, 2024 and 2025; AIVD/MIVD/NCTV `Dreigingsbeeld Statelijke Actoren` 1 (2021) and 2 (2022); AIVD/NCTV `Over de grens` (2024).
- MIVD openbare jaarverslagen 2024 and 2025.
- NCTV `Dreigingsbeeld Terrorisme Nederland` December 2025; WODC/NCTV `National Risk Assessment Terrorismefinanciering 2019`.
- The parliamentary interrogation report `(On)zichtbare invloed` (POCOB, Kamerstuk 35 228 nr. 4, 25 June 2020); Kamerstukken 30 821 nr. 257, 325 and 337; Kamerstuk 35 646 (Wtmo).
- Dutch statutes: Wet financiering politieke partijen (Wfpp), consolidated text 6 June 2026.
- Court judgments: Rechtbank Den Haag 3 June 2003 (as reported by the AIVD); Rechtbank Rotterdam 27 May 2026, ECLI:NL:RBROT:2026:6051.
- EU designations: Council Decision 2003/902/EC; Council Regulation (EC) 881/2002, consolidated 4 April 2023; Regulation (EU) 2024/386.
- UN Security Council consolidated sanctions list, generated 20 November 2026.
- Tweede Kamer lobbyists register, 2 November 2026; BZK letter on the lobby register, 8 May 2026.
- Commissariaat voor de Media, `Mediamonitor 2025` (data as at 31 December 2024).

### 1.2 Rules applied

- Every finding is quoted verbatim in the language of the source. Dutch sources are quoted in Dutch. EU sources are quoted in the official Dutch version where one exists. The UN list is English; a Dutch rendering is marked as a translation.
- No finding is aggregated into a total that the source does not itself give.
- No Dutch person or company is described as connected to a foreign state or to extremism unless the cited authority named them. Where a newspaper alleged something and no authority confirmed it, the item is in §6 and labelled **ALLEGATION**, with the outlet, date and any denial found.
- “Does not claim” is part of every item, not a disclaimer at the end.

### 1.3 Limits

- The AIVD, MIVD and NCTV publish what they choose to publish. Absence from a public report is not proof of absence of activity; it is proof that the authority has not publicly established it.
- POCOB’s evidence is partly testimony and partly documents obtained by a parliamentary committee. Its findings are official but are not criminal convictions.
- The 2003 Al-Aqsa judgment is a civil interlocutory judgment (kort geding). The 2026 Al-Aqsa judgment is criminal. They answer different questions.
- EU and UN list entries current on 20 November 2026 were checked for Netherlands addresses and Dutch nationality. No claim is made about classified designations.
- The report does not audit every Kamerstuk. It reads the ones that carry findings on these channels.

## 2. State influence on Dutch politics

### 2.1 Russia

#### Item 1 — AIVD/MIVD/NCTV, `Dreigingsbeeld Statelijke Actoren 2`, November 2022 (published 2 November 2022)

**Quote (p. 19):**

> “Russische inspanningen richten zich onder meer op het creëren van een voor Rusland welgevallig beeld: zowel in het publieke debat als in het politiek-bestuurlijke stelsel. Daarnaast zijn de Russische inspanningen erop gericht de politieke eenheid in het Westen te ondermijnen, onder meer door bestaande tegenstellingen te versterken. Hierbij beperkt Rusland zich niet tot één specifieke politieke stroming in Europa, maar zoekt het in plaats daarvan opportunistisch naar kansen voor heimelijke politieke beïnvloeding in het Westen.”

> “Zo worden in Nederland pogingen ondernomen om twijfel te zaaien aangaande zowel de toedracht van het neerhalen van vlucht MH17, als van het MH17-strafproces.”

**URL:** https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/ronl-f76b037c88b27bbede038d38647642b408245240/pdf

**Claims:** Russia conducts covert political influence aimed at the public debate and the political-administrative system; it is opportunistic rather than tied to one Dutch party; Dutch-specific activity includes attempts to cast doubt on MH17 and the MH17 trial.

**Does not claim:** It names no Dutch person, party, foundation or media outlet. It gives no funding amounts and does not say any Dutch political party received Russian money.

#### Item 2 — AIVD, `Jaarverslag 2023`, 22 April 2024

**Quote (p. 27):**

> “Buitenlandse inlichtingendiensten waren afgelopen jaar in Nederland actief om de publieke opinie of politieke besluiten te beïnvloeden. Zo had Rusland in 2023 de hand in demonstraties tegen westerse steun aan Oekraïne, die plaatsvonden in Nederland en andere westerse landen.”

> “Ook de inlichtingendiensten van enkele andere landen waren in Nederland actief om politieke besluitvorming te beïnvloeden in hun eigen belang. Daarbij viel op dat die inspanningen niet alleen gericht waren tegen de landelijke overheid, maar soms ook tegen een lokale overheid.”

**URL:** https://www.aivd.nl/documenten/2024/04/22/jaarverslag-2023

**Claims:** Foreign intelligence services were active in the Netherlands to influence public opinion or political decisions; Russia was involved in demonstrations against Western support for Ukraine; other unnamed states also targeted political decision-making, including at local level.

**Does not claim:** No Dutch party, politician, demonstration organiser or funder is named. The finding is about activity, not about illegal funding or criminal liability.

#### Item 3 — AIVD, `Jaarverslag 2024`, 24 April 2025

**Quote (p. 26):**

> “In Nederland hebben geen vergelijkbare incidenten plaatsgevonden, maar de AIVD en de MIVD hebben wel cyberoperaties en voorbereidende handelingen onderkend die mogelijk tot sabotage hadden kunnen leiden.”

**URL:** https://www.aivd.nl/documenten/2025/04/24/jaarverslag-2024

**Claims:** Unlike Poland and the Baltic states, the Netherlands did not see comparable Russian sabotage incidents in 2024; cyber operations and preparatory acts were observed.

**Does not claim:** It does not establish a completed Russian sabotage attack in the Netherlands, nor a link to Dutch political parties.

#### Item 4 — AIVD, `Jaarverslag 2025`, 23 April 2026

**Quote (p. 44):**

> “De Russische diensten zoeken toenadering tot parlementariërs in Europese lidstaten. Dit doen zij zowel heimelijk als in de openheid. Openlijk nodigt Moskou parlementariërs uit om bijvoorbeeld deel te nemen aan evenementen in en buiten Rusland.”

> “De AIVD heeft briefings gegeven aan Tweede Kamerleden en Nederlandse Europarlementariërs over de dreiging die uitgaat van Russische heimelijke beïnvloeding.”

**URL:** https://aivd.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2026/04/23/jaarverslag-2025/aivd-jaarverslag-2025.pdf

**Claims:** Russian services approach European parliamentarians, openly and covertly; the AIVD treated the threat as serious enough to brief Dutch MPs and MEPs directly.

**Does not claim:** No MP, MEP or party is named as having accepted an approach, received money, or acted on Russian instructions.

#### Item 5 — MIVD, `Openbaar jaarverslag 2024`, 22 April 2025

**Quote (p. 10):**

> “Dit uit zich bijvoorbeeld in de vorm van zowel fysieke als digitale (klassieke) spionage, (heimelijke) beïnvloeding van het publiek debat, het politiek-bestuurlijk bestel en diplomatie, offensieve cyberaanvallen en campagnes, sabotage en de inzet van energiebeleid als pressiemiddel.”

**Quote (p. 14):**

> “In 2024 voerden dergelijke groeperingen DDoS-aanvallen uit tegen onder meer websites van politieke partijen en openbaarvervoersbedrijven in Nederland, in een poging het Nederlanders moeilijk te maken hun stem uit te brengen tijdens de Europese verkiezingen.”

**URL:** https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/2025/04/22/mivd-openbaar-jaarverslag-2024

**Claims:** Russia uses covert influence of the public debate and the political-administrative system; pro-Russian hacktivists attacked Dutch political-party websites during the 2024 European election.

**Does not claim:** It does not name the parties whose websites were attacked, the attackers, or any Dutch person or company as a collaborator.

#### Item 6 — MIVD, `Openbaar jaarverslag 2025`, 21 April 2026

**Quote (p. 5):**

> “Daarnaast zien we dat Rusland ook cyberaanvallen, desinformatiecampagnes, sabotageacties en spionage inzet om angst, onrust en verdeeldheid te veroorzaken en besluitvorming te beïnvloeden.”

**URL:** https://eerstekamer.nl/eu/overig/20260421/openbaar_jaarverslag_2025/document3/f=/vmxncog795wg.pdf

**Claims:** Russia’s toolkit is aimed at decision-making, not only at military targets.

**Does not claim:** No Dutch decision, party or person is identified as successfully influenced.

### 2.2 China

#### Item 7 — AIVD/MIVD/NCTV, `Dreigingsbeeld Statelijke Actoren 2`, November 2022

**Quote (p. 20):**

> “Hoewel er in Nederland grootschalige vergaring van persoonsgegevens door China is waargenomen, zijn er vooralsnog geen aanwijzingen voor gerichte Chinese beïnvloedingsactiviteiten tegen Nederlandse doelwitten.”

**URL:** https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/ronl-f76b037c88b27bbede038d38647642b408245240/pdf

**Claims:** China collected personal data on a large scale in the Netherlands; as of November 2022 the services saw no indications of targeted Chinese influence against Dutch targets.

**Does not claim:** It does not say China never attempted influence; it says the services had no indications of *targeted* influence against Dutch targets at that time. It names no Dutch target.

#### Item 8 — AIVD, `Jaarverslag 2024`, 24 April 2025

**Quote (p. 33):**

> “Met deze activiteiten wil het regime de politieke besluiten van andere landen beïnvloeden, zodat die gunstiger uitpakken voor China. Het regime zet daarvoor ook Chinese emigranten in.”

> “Op basis van de Chinese wetgeving en whole-of-societybenadering kan iedere staatsburger, ook in het buitenland, gedwongen worden samen te werken met de Chinese overheid.”

**URL:** https://www.aivd.nl/documenten/2025/04/24/jaarverslag-2024

**Claims:** China seeks to influence other countries’ political decisions; it uses Chinese emigrants and can compel citizens abroad to cooperate.

**Does not claim:** No Dutch party, politician, civil servant or foundation is named as an instrument. It does not quantify Chinese funding in the Netherlands.

#### Item 9 — AIVD, `Jaarverslag 2025`, 23 April 2026

**Quote (p. 49):**

> “Daarnaast probeert China op heimelijke wijze politieke invloed uit te oefenen in het buitenland. Dit werd in 2025 in verschillende westerse landen zichtbaar.”

> “Ook voor het uitoefenen van politieke invloed buiten eigen grenzen gebruikt China onder meer de Chinese diaspora in Nederland, al dan niet onder dwang.”

**URL:** https://aivd.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2026/04/23/jaarverslag-2025/aivd-jaarverslag-2025.pdf

**Claims:** China covertly seeks political influence abroad and uses the Chinese diaspora in the Netherlands, sometimes under coercion.

**Does not claim:** No Dutch person or organisation is named as a Chinese proxy; no funding amount or party link is given.

#### Item 10 — AIVD/NCTV, `Over de grens. Statelijke inmenging in diasporagemeenschappen in Nederland`, 17 October 2024

**Quote (p. 16):**

> “De Chinese overheid biedt haar ingezetenen studiebeurzen voor het buitenland. In Nederland wordt dat geregeld door de China Scholarship Council (CSC), een non-profitorganisatie die gelieerd is aan het Chinese ministerie van Onderwijs.”

> “De CSC stelt verschillende eisen aan het verkrijgen van zo’n beurs, die de vrijheden van de betreffende studenten in ons land inperken, waaronder toezeggingen van loyaliteit van de student aan de Chinese Communistische Partij, terugkeer- en garantieclausules, en clausules over begeleiding door en/of gehoorzaamheid aan de Chinese ambassade of het Chinese consulaat in het land van bestemming.”

**URL:** https://www.aivd.nl/documenten/2024/10/17/over-de-grens-statelijke-inmenging-in-diasporagemeenschappen-in-nederland

**Claims:** A Chinese state-linked scholarship body operates in the Netherlands and imposes loyalty, return and embassy-supervision conditions on students.

**Does not claim:** It does not say the CSC funds Dutch think tanks or political parties, and it does not quantify the number of students affected.

### 2.3 Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, Qatar)

#### Item 11 — AIVD/MIVD/NCTV, `Dreigingsbeeld Statelijke Actoren` 1, 3 February 2021

**Quote (pp. 23–24):**

> “Vanuit een aantal islamitische landen, waaronder Qatar, Koeweit en Saoedi-Arabië, worden religieuze groepen en organisaties van wahabi salafistische signatuur in het buitenland gefinancierd.”

> “De mate waarin sprake is van verwevenheid met overheden verschilt per land en varieert van directe aansturing tot passief gedogen.”

**URL:** https://www.aivd.nl/documenten/2021/02/03/dreigingsbeeld-statelijke-actoren-dbsa

**Claims:** Qatar, Kuwait and Saudi Arabia fund Wahhabi-Salafi religious groups abroad; the degree of state involvement ranges from direct steering to passive tolerance.

**Does not claim:** It names no Dutch mosque or foundation, gives no amounts, and does not say the funded organisations were terrorist organisations.

#### Item 12 — AIVD, `Jaarverslag 2023`, 22 April 2024

**Quote (p. 10):**

> “De beweging werd in 2023 nauwelijks gefinancierd of beïnvloed door geldschieters uit de Golfregio. Wahhabi-salafistische organisaties leken beter in staat in eigen kring geld inzamelen. Tegelijk leken donoren uit de Golfstaten minder geneigd voor projecten in Nederland te betalen.”

**URL:** https://www.aivd.nl/documenten/2024/04/22/jaarverslag-2023

**Claims:** In 2023 the Wahhabi-Salafi movement in the Netherlands was barely financed or influenced by Gulf funders; it raised money in its own circle; Gulf donors appeared less willing to pay for Dutch projects.

**Does not claim:** It does not say Gulf funding was zero, nor that it had never existed. It is a 2023 observation, not a time series.

#### Item 13 — AIVD/NCTV, `Over de grens`, 17 October 2024

**Quote (p. 15):**

> “Via de diaspora kan een overheid invloed uitoefenen op de publieke opinie en politiek in het buitenland.”

> “Personen en organisaties binnen diasporagemeenschappen in Nederland kunnen door buitenlandse overheden worden gebruikt om onze besluitvormingsprocessen op een ongewenste manier te beïnvloeden.”

**Quote (p. 26):**

> “Daarbij hebben buitenlandse overheden in het bijzonder interesse voor bestuurders, politici of politieke organisaties met banden of wortels in hun eigen land.”

> “Met de hulp van deze ‘politieke bondgenoten’ wordt de politieke of publieke opinie in andere landen of EU-breed over relevante kwesties beïnvloed met eigen standpunten. Zij dienen bijvoorbeeld resoluties in of brengen documenten onder de aandacht van Europarlementariërs of nationale politici.”

**Quote (p. 16):**

> “Uit onderzoek werd duidelijk dat veel Eritreeërs (sociale) druk ervaren om de ‘diasporataks’ te betalen die de Eritrese autoriteiten (laten) innen onder hun diaspora in Nederland.”

**URL:** https://www.aivd.nl/documenten/2024/10/17/over-de-grens-statelijke-inmenging-in-diasporagemeenschappen-in-nederland

**Claims:** Diaspora communities are a channel for foreign states to influence Dutch public opinion and political decisions; Eritrean authorities collect a diaspora tax under social pressure.

**Does not claim:** The Eritrean example is a funding channel, not a finding that the money financed terrorism or a Dutch party. No Dutch political party is named.

### 2.7 United States and Israel: documented absence

#### Item 20 — No Dutch authority finding of US or Israeli influence on Dutch politics in the examined corpus

**Method:** Full-text search of the AIVD 2023–2025 reports, DBSA 1–2, MIVD 2024–2025, `Over de grens`, DTN December 2025, POCOB and the Kamerstukken cited here for “Verenigde Staten”, “VS”, “Israël” and “Israëlisch”. The documents discuss the US and Israel as geopolitical actors, allies, or sources of intelligence in a court case — not as states conducting influence operations against Dutch politics.

**Illustration (AIVD 2025, p. 5):** the US appears as a geopolitical actor:

> “Met overweldigend militair optreden in Venezuela en Iran heeft de Verenigde Staten duidelijk gemaakt dat het zal ingrijpen als het vindt dat andere landen Amerika’s positie bedreigen, of als zij Amerika’s zwaarwegende belangen in gevaar brengen.”

**URL:** https://aivd.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2026/04/23/jaarverslag-2025/aivd-jaarverslag-2025.pdf

**Claims:** The AIVD treats the US as a geopolitical power whose actions shape the threat environment.

**Does not claim:** It does not describe US influence on Dutch political parties, elections, think tanks or media. The same is true for Israel in the documents examined.

**Judicial footnote (not an influence finding):** In the 2026 Al-Aqsa criminal judgment, the Rotterdam court declined to rely on expert reports that were based largely on news articles and on US and Israeli security-service material, because it could not exclude political colouring. That is a ruling on evidence reliability, not a finding that Israel influenced Dutch politics. See Item 27.

## 3. Funding of extremist and anti-democratic groups

### 3.1 Hamas and Al-Aqsa

#### Item 21 — AIVD, `Jaarverslag 2025`, 23 April 2026

**Quote (p. 23):**

> “Ten eerste is in Europa al jaren een ‘politiek’ netwerk actief dat financiële steun voor Hamas verwerft en zich in Europa bezighoudt met activisme, lobby- en andere beïnvloedingswerkzaamheden. In Nederland is een tiental personen actief dat aan dit Europese netwerk te verbinden is. Dit Hamas-netwerk in Nederland is ook al jaren actief met propaganda, lobbywerk en fondsenwerving voor Hamas (in Gaza).”

> “Ten opzichte van 2024 heeft de AIVD meer duidelijkheid gekregen over de betrokkenheid van het Hamas-netwerk in Nederland bij pro-Palestina- en anti-Israëlprotesten.”

**URL:** https://aivd.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2026/04/23/jaarverslag-2025/aivd-jaarverslag-2025.pdf

**Claims:** A Hamas-linked political network operates in Europe; about ten people in the Netherlands are connected; it conducts propaganda, lobbying and fundraising for Hamas in Gaza and is involved in broader pro-Palestinian organising.

**Does not claim:** It names no person or organisation, gives no amounts, and does not say the network is funded by a foreign state. “Lobbywerk” here is Hamas-linked advocacy, not registration under a Dutch lobby register.

#### Item 22 — Rechtbank Den Haag, 3 June 2003 (civil interlocutory judgment, as reported by the AIVD)

**Quote:**

**URL:** https://uitspraken.rechtspraak.nl/details?id=ECLI:NL:RBROT:2026:6051

**Claims:** The court convicted the operator for leading the evasion of sanctions and continuing the banned Stichting Al Aqsa; it acquitted him of providing funds to Hamas because it could not establish that the recipient charities were controlled by Hamas or that the money reached Hamas.

### 3.4 POCOB: foreign funding of mosques and religious foundations

#### Item 30 — POCOB, `(On)zichtbare invloed`, 25 June 2020, p. 9

**Quote (p. 9):**

> “De commissie constateert dat financiering vanuit onvrije landen bijna uitsluitend plaatsvindt aan organisaties die de religieus-politieke visie van de donateur aanhangen; er vindt dus voorselectie plaats.”

**Quote (p. 10):**

**URL:** https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2020D25817

**Claims:** No single Dutch government body has visibility of all foreign money flows.

**Does not claim:** It does not say no body has partial visibility; it says none has complete visibility. This is the factual basis for the later Wtmo proposal.

#### Item 32 — POCOB, p. 9: donors linked to terrorist organisations

**Quote:**

> “Het is veelal onduidelijk wat de motieven zijn van de donateur. Zo heeft de commissie diverse voorbeelden gezien van donaties van organisaties die gelinkt worden aan terreurorganisaties, zoals de International Islamic Charitable Organization (IICO) uit Koeweit.”

**URL:** https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2020D25817

**Claims:** POCOB saw examples of donations from organisations linked to terrorist organisations, naming IICO (Kuwait).

**Does not claim:** It does not find that the recipient mosque or foundation was itself a terrorist organisation, nor that the donation financed terrorism. The finding is about donor links, not recipient culpability.

#### Item 33 — POCOB, p. 109: alFitrah’s controversy is not a finding

**Quote:**

> “Deze stichting is meermalen in opspraak geraakt, met name vanwege mogelijke relaties met omstreden (terroristische) organisaties, verdenking van witwaspraktijken, omstreden gastpredikers en vooral vanwege de inhoud van het informele moskeeonderwijs dat gegeven wordt bij Dar al-Hudaa.”

**URL:** https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2020D25817

**Claims:** POCOB records that alFitrah was repeatedly in the news over *possible* relations with controversial (terrorist) organisations, suspected money laundering and the content of its informal education.

**Does not claim:** The words “mogelijke” (possible) and “verdenking” (suspicion) are the finding. POCOB did not convict alFitrah of terrorism or money laundering. This is the exact sentence that secondary sources turn into “alFitrah had terrorist links”.

## 6. Allegations not confirmed by any Dutch authority

The following are included only because the public debate treats them as findings. They are **ALLEGATION**, not established fact. Dutch authorities did not confirm them in the documents examined.

### 6.1 Voice of Europe and a Dutch MEP

**ALLEGATION — NRC, 28 March 2024 and 29 May 2024.** NRC reported that the Prague-based outlet Voice of Europe was a platform for pro-Russian politicians and that police conducted a search at the address of an assistant to FvD MEP Marcel de Graaff in connection with a Russian influence investigation. The Czech government alleged that politicians had been paid; in the subsequent parliamentary debate the Dutch government did not name any politician.

**What is established by an authority:** On 27 May 2024 the EU sanctioned Voice of Europe and two individuals, Artem Marchevskyi and Viktor Medvedchuk, for information manipulation. The EU did **not** sanction De Graaff. No Dutch court judgment or Dutch security-service report in the examined corpus names him as paid by Russia.

**Denial:** De Graaff denied receiving Russian money (AD, 29 May 2024; de Volkskrant, 13 April 2024). FvD has denied being pro-Russian (de Volkskrant, 19 September 2026).

## 7. Plain statement of what is not established

These are results, not gaps to be filled by inference.

1. **This research found no Dutch authority naming a Dutch political party as funded by a foreign state.** The Wfpp bans non-Dutch gifts (Art. 23a), and the government says it can fine violations, but this research found no published enforcement decision or court judgment finding such a payment.
2. **This research found no Dutch authority naming a Dutch think tank as foreign-funded.** POCOB’s findings concern mosques and religious foundations; the Wtmo, which would have covered civil-society organisations, was rejected on 24 March 2026. Independent think tanks are not covered by the Wfpp ban.
3. **No public register of foreign-state lobbying at the Tweede Kamer exists.** The House’s register is a passholder list (34 entries on 2 November 2026), not a meetings register; the statutory lobby register was still being designed in May 2026.
4. **This research found no Dutch authority recording foreign state ownership of Dutch media.** The Commissariaat voor de Media records Belgian and German commercial owners; Talpa’s ownership is “Onbekend”.
5. **This research found no Dutch authority naming the United States or Israel as a state influencing Dutch politics.** They appear as geopolitical actors, allies, or (in one court ruling) sources of evidence the court declined to rely on. That is not an influence finding.
6. **POCOB did not find that the mosques it studied were terrorist organisations.** It found foreign funding, donor links to terrorist organisations, and a transparency gap. Its alFitrah passage says “mogelijke relaties” and “verdenking”, not conviction.
7. **The 2026 Al-Aqsa criminal judgment did not find that Al-Aqsa money reached Hamas.** It convicted on sanctions evasion and continuing a banned organisation, and acquitted on the Hamas-funding count for insufficient evidence. The 2003 civil judgment had accepted the AIVD’s intelligence-based conclusion. Both are official; they answer different legal questions.
8. **The RIHS designation does not cover the whole organisation.** UN and EU designate only the Pakistan and Afghanistan offices. The AIVD information relayed by POCOB is therefore not a “designated entity funded As-Soennah” finding.
9. **AIVD 2023 says Gulf funding of the Wahhabi-Salafi movement was “nauwelijks” in 2023**, not that it never existed and not that it was zero. The 2021 DBSA and the 2020 POCOB report document earlier funding.
10. **This research found no formal parliamentary inquiry (enquête) on this subject.** POCOB was an ondervragingscommissie; its report is `(On)zichtbare invloed`, Kamerstuk 35 228 nr. 4, 25 June 2020.

## 9. One-line conclusion

The Dutch primary record documents Russian covert political influence, Chinese political espionage and diaspora pressure, Gulf and Turkish funding of religious infrastructure, and a Hamas-linked fundraising/lobbying network — but it does **not** document foreign-state funding of a Dutch political party or think tank, or foreign-state ownership of a Dutch media outlet, and it does **not** support the common shorthand that Al-Aqsa was criminally convicted of financing Hamas.
